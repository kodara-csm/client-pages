About Reynold Thomas

An engineer who became a teacher, and never stopped looking for potential.

I help GCSE and A Level students, and their parents, see what they are truly capable of in Maths, then get them there.

My story

Someone saw potential in me before I had the qualification. I have been paying it forward ever since.

I graduated with an Engineering degree and started my career as an IT Engineering Technician. After the dotcom recession in 2000, I moved into education as a Design and Technology Technician in a secondary school.

School leaders gave me the chance to teach before I held a formal teaching qualification. That was uncommon, and their confidence in me is what made me believe I could make a genuine difference. The school then supported me through a Master's degree and Graduate Teacher Training.

The lesson that changed how I teach

My early years in the classroom were not easy. Like many new teachers, I focused on subject knowledge and lesson delivery. Over time I learned that great teaching depends just as much on relationships: understanding each learner, building trust and adapting to how they learn. When I did that, behaviour and engagement changed, and so did results.

What I do now

I have spent over 20 years in secondary education, further education and sixth form colleges, lecturing in mathematics, engineering and related subjects. Alongside that I built a private tutoring practice in GCSE Maths, GCSE Further Maths, GCSE Physics, AS Maths and A Level Maths, which has grown almost entirely through referrals.

I have seen a student capped at grade 5 on the Foundation tier move to Higher and achieve an A. I have seen a Year 8 student sit GCSE Maths in Year 9 and achieve an A, and go on towards a career in medicine.

I do not simply teach subjects. I identify potential, remove barriers to success and help learners achieve outcomes they once believed were beyond their reach.
Reynold Thomas

How I work

Assess the potential first. Then close the gaps.

Five stages: assess potential, not just current performance. Diagnose the specific knowledge gaps. Build confidence and motivation. Develop mastery through structured learning. Measure progress and raise expectations. What I deliberately do not do is accept a predicted grade or a tier as the final word.
