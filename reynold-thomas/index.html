A note to parents

A predicted grade is a starting point, not a ceiling.

Picture your child walking into their maths exam knowing exactly what they need to do. No panic, no guessing which topics will come up. Just the quiet confidence of someone who has already done the hard part.

That is the outcome I work towards with every student, and for most of them it is closer than anyone thinks.

Many students are underestimated, not incapable.

Too often, learners are labelled by predicted grades, ability groups, exam tiers or past results. Those labels can be useful, but they can also become artificial ceilings. A student on the GCSE Foundation tier, for example, can never achieve above a grade 5, however well they do on the day.

One student came to me in exactly that position. After my own assessment, I told his parents he was capable of much more. They asked the school for a reassessment, the school agreed and moved him to the Higher tier, and after continued tutoring he achieved an A.

Every learner deserves to be assessed on their potential, not simply their current performance.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Can a student really move from Foundation to Higher tier?

Yes. Schools can reassess a student's tier, and I have seen it happen. The key is evidence of what the student can actually do, which is exactly what a proper assessment gives you.

My child understands it in class but struggles in exams. Why?

It is one of the questions parents ask me most. Usually there are a few specific gaps, plus confidence and exam technique. Find those and the grade can move faster than you would expect.

Does my child need tutoring, or a different approach?

Often it is the approach. Covering chapters your child already understands does not move the grade. Targeting the 2 or 3 gaps that hold everything else back does.
